# Design System: House Stock

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "One Stock, Many Rooms"**

House Stock is a design system for web tools, internal interfaces, documentation and the occasional expressive showcase. Every surface is printed on the same warm stock with the same inks: Cooper for titles, Sabon Next for reading and working text, PragmataPro for notation, and a single rust or amber accent. What changes from room to room is how much of the vocabulary a surface uses, not the material it's made of.

The system is a specification, not a component library. Adopting apps implement it in their own stack. Scope and ownership live in `PRODUCT.md`.

### Principles

1. **Warm stock, always.** Neutrals are cream and brown-black, never pure white, pure black or cool grey. Ink is warm too.
2. **One accent per edition.** Rust in daylight, amber in the evening. It marks what can be followed, focused or chosen. Semantic and content colors stay inside their own roles.
3. **Type carries the character.** Personality comes from Cooper, Sabon small caps, mono notation and careful rules, not from effects, gradients or costumes.
4. **The task sets the layout.** Measure, density, navigation and hierarchy follow what people are doing on the surface. A reading column is not an app width.
5. **Physical only when it means something.** Paper objects, tilt, shadows and fastenings show up when something really is an object. They never decorate a working control.
6. **Accessibility isn't negotiable.** Contrast, keyboard use, visible focus, text enlargement, reduced motion and forced colors work in every mode and both editions.

### Surface modes

Each screen, or each clear region of one, chooses a mode. The rest of this document says what each mode uses.

| Mode         | For                                          | Character                                                                                                                          |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Working**  | Tools, dashboards, settings, editors, admin  | Flat tonal layers, Sabon hierarchy, 2–6px corners, quiet shadows only on raised things, labels chosen by role, dense where useful. |
| **Reading**  | Docs, help, posts, long explanations         | Measured column, generous line height, rule-led sections; drop caps, fleurons and footnote treatments allowed.                     |
| **Showcase** | Landing pages, profiles, launches, galleries | Cooper section titles, poster-scale type, physical paper objects, asymmetric grids, expressive motion.                             |

A Working app can include a Reading help page; a Showcase page can include a Working form. Don't blend the modes inside a single component.

## Colors

There are two editions. **Daylight** is cream stock with brown ink and Marquee Rust. **Evening** is brown-black stock with chalky ink and Footlight Amber. Frontmatter holds the values. Components use the semantic roles below, never edition literals.

### Accent

The accent marks interaction: links and their underlines, focus, current navigation, selected filters, the primary action. In Reading and Showcase modes it may also color a drop cap, a fleuron, one emphasized word in a title, a thin stripe on a ticket or the TOC, or a solid ticket end. Don't color every heading or panel. Don't use the accent for body text.

Strokes are tested as text on both their tint and the page, so they can carry short status labels. Always pair state color with a word or icon. These hues are for state, not decoration; they never style navigation or ordinary headings.

### Theme behavior

Each app chooses daylight-only, evening-only, or both with a stored preference and a system default, and records that choice locally. When both are offered:

- apply the saved choice before first paint, with no flash;
- keep `color-scheme`, native controls, `theme-color`, syntax styles and images in step with the active edition;
- give both editions the same capabilities and hierarchy.

## Typography

- **Cooper\*** with Georgia: titles, ornaments, drop caps. Never body copy, controls or labels.
- **Sabon Next** with Georgia and Times New Roman: reading, working text, controls, small-caps labels.
- **PragmataPro Mono Liga** with platform monospace: code, literals, kickers and machine metadata.

### Scale by mode

| Role                  | Working                 | Reading                                | Showcase                              |
| --------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Page title            | `display`               | `display`                              | `display-poster` or `headline-poster` |
| Section title         | `headline` (Sabon)      | `headline` (Sabon)                     | `title-section` (Cooper)              |
| Subsection            | `title`                 | `title`                                | `title-feature` or `title`            |
| Body                  | `ui`, `body` for prose  | `body-reading`, `body-lead` for intros | `body`, `body-lead`                   |
| Controls, table cells | `ui`, `ui-small`        | `ui`                                   | `ui`                                  |
| Labels                | `label` or `mono-label` | `label`                                | `kicker`, `label`                     |
| Drop cap, fleuron     | No                      | Yes                                    | Yes                                   |

`title-feature` names the title of a featured object: a card, a project, a show, a release. Poster scale is for pages where the title is the content, never above a recurring task.

### Voices

- **Small-caps labels** (`label`): navigation, chips, short headings, field groups. Use real small caps.
- **Kickers** (`kicker`): tracked uppercase mono above a title or on a ticket. One or two words.
- **Mono labels** (`mono-label`): counts, hashes, units, file types, timestamps.
- **Literals** (`code`): code, paths, identifiers, handles, numeric data that benefits from alignment. Logs and code are real content; prompt characters and fake terminals as decoration are not.
- **Meta** (`meta`): secondary reading text, often italic. Muted, never faint.
- **Drop cap and fleuron:** a three-line Cooper drop cap in accent and a single ❦ divider, for Reading and Showcase only.

Pick one label voice per role and keep it for the whole app.

**The Playbill Rule.** Cooper is the title and ornament voice. Everything you read at length or operate is Sabon or mono.

### Assets

Fonts are hosted at `fonts.finnrut.is`. Cooper\* is OFL; Sabon Next and PragmataPro are licensed. Before an app loads them, confirm its domain is covered by the licenses and by the font host's CORS policy. Never redistribute licensed files. Check rendering with the intended faces and with fallbacks only; both must hold the hierarchy.

## Layout

| Mode         | Structure                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Working**  | Sidebars, tabs, split panes, toolbars and wide tables as the task needs. Up to `layout-shell` or full width. Prose inside the workspace still respects `layout-measure`.  |
| **Reading**  | Single column at `layout-measure` (43.75rem), `body-reading` line height, `layout-section` between major parts, rules between groups. A side TOC is fine on wide screens. |
| **Showcase** | Centered `layout-shell` (75rem), asymmetric label/content splits, 12-column spreads, varied object placement, `layout-section` rhythm.                                    |

- **Spacing:** use the size scale (`3xs`–`2xl`) for gaps and padding. `layout-*` tokens are for page structure only.
- **Responsive:** break at content pressure, in `em`, so text enlargement reflows like zoom. Use container queries for components that pair text with a fixed-width column.
- **Overflow:** no page-level horizontal scroll. Wide tables, code and logs scroll in their own labelled, keyboard-focusable region. Code blocks cap at `code-max-height` in Reading mode; log viewers in Working mode can use the full panel.
- **Targets:** standalone controls are at least `target-min` (44px) high without overlapping neighbors. Inline links stay inline.

**The Words Stay Level Rule.** Tilt paper and artwork, never live headings, captions, labels or data. Lettering printed on a rotating object may move with it, as long as the same information exists as level, selectable text nearby.

## Elevation & Depth

| Mode         | Depth                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Working**  | Flat. Tone (`--paper-shade` on `--paper`) and rules separate groups. `quiet` shadow only on things that genuinely sit above: menus, popovers, dragged items, lifted chips. |
| **Reading**  | Flat, plus `code-card` on code blocks and `quiet` on lifted chips. A faint page-level radial light at the top and shade at the bottom is allowed.                          |
| **Showcase** | Physical. Prints, portraits, sheets and tickets use the `print-*`, `portrait-light`, `sheet` and `cut-object` recipes. Loose prints lift on hover and focus.               |

Each edition sets `--shadow` from `shadow-color-light` or `shadow-color-dark`. Evening physical objects use `print-dark` and `print-lifted-dark`, which add a contact edge because a dark shadow alone disappears on dark stock.

Clipped shapes (torn tickets, slips) lose their own `box-shadow`. Put `cut-object` on an unclipped wrapper as a `filter`.

Page lighting suggests light falling on stock. No colored spotlights, glows, glass or gradient fills on components.

**The Paper Casts, Words Don't Rule.** Shadows belong to objects and raised surfaces, never to text, rules, icons or drawn marks.

## Shapes

- **Interface pieces:** `hairline` (2px) alerts; `sm` (3px) inline code, images, small buttons; `md` (4px) code blocks, inputs, search, TOC; `card` (6px) cards, comments, dialogs; `pill` for chips, badges and tags.
- **Paper objects:** square sheets, mats and cards; tickets with torn ends and dashed perforations; irregular backing shapes. Resting angles stay within `resting-tilt` (±4°) and vary between neighbors. A single larger overlap angle is fine as a Showcase accent.

Choose shape by what the thing is, not by mode or edition. Never clip a field, hit area or focus ring into a decorative edge.

## Components

These are patterns for apps to implement, not installed components.

### Navigation and links

- **Text links:** accent with a visible underline. Hover moves to `--accent-hover` and thickens the underline over `fast`.
- **Navigation:** Working uses a sidebar, tab row or top bar with `label` or `ui` text. Reading uses a quiet small-caps row. Showcase may back items with paper slips that appear on hover, focus and current.
- **Current location:** marked visibly (accent, slip, rule or weight) and with `aria-current`, never by hover alone.
- **Arrows:** → in-app, ↗ external or opens a document, ↓ download or jump down, ← back. Always with words; an arrow isn't a label.

### Buttons

- **Quiet button:** transparent or `--paper-shade`, `--ink` or `--muted` label, `sm` corners, accent on hover and focus.
- **Primary button:** `--accent` fill with `--on-accent` text, moving to `--accent-hover` on hover. One per view in Working mode.
- **Danger button:** `--alert-caution` stroke and text on paper; fill only inside a confirmation step.
- Use `<button>` for actions and `<a>` for navigation and downloads.

### Forms

Fields look like working controls, not collectibles. Use `--paper` or `--ticket-stock` fill, `--border-strong` boundary at `control` width, `md` corners, `ui` text and a visible label above. Placeholders never replace labels. Native controls are fine.

States: default, hover, focus, selected, disabled, invalid, pending. Each one is visible without color (icon, text, weight or pattern). Error text sits with its field, says what went wrong and how to fix it, and is linked with `aria-describedby`. Pending actions block duplicates while keeping their status visible.

### Focus

Use `--focus` everywhere. `focus-compact` in dense Working UI, `focus-roomy` where there's space, `focus-inset` for adjacent tiles. Never clipped, never removed, always visible on every surface it can land on.

### Lists, tables and panels

Ruled lists and label/content splits come before cards. Tables keep real headers; sorting and filtering show their current state. Use `mono-label` or `code` for literal values and right-align numbers. Panels use `--paper-shade` and `card` corners; static panels never lift as if clickable. Dialogs and menus use `--ticket-stock`, `card` corners and `quiet` shadow, with proper focus trapping, Escape to close and focus return.

### Code and logs

Inline code: `--paper-shade`, `sm` corners. Blocks: `--code-stock`, `md` corners, `code-card` shadow, an optional filename or language header in `mono-label`, optional line numbers and highlighted lines. Copy is a labelled button with success and failure feedback. Long lines scroll inside the block. Gruvbox is the default syntax theme in both editions; any replacement must pass contrast on `--code-stock`.

### Chips, tags and alerts

- **Chips:** `pill`, `--paper-shade`, `--border` stroke, `label` text, optional `mono-label` count. Static tags and selectable filters look different; a selected filter has a non-color indicator and `aria-pressed` or equivalent.
- **Alerts:** `alert-stripe` left border in the state stroke, state tint behind, `hairline` corners, bold first line, an action when one is needed.
- **Link directory card:** a `card`-cornered `--paper-shade` tile with title, one-line description and arrow, lifting by `lift` on hover.
- **Reading aids:** a TOC with an accent stripe on the current entry, disclosure summaries in `label`, footnotes that highlight with `--selection-bg` when targeted.

### Footer

A quiet row of outline icons at `target-min`, or a link directory with paper slips behind hover, focus and current. A pill badge may show a real commit hash. Never invent build data, and never expose details the app's access boundary shouldn't.

### Physical objects (Showcase)

Patterns, each with its own logic:

- **Mounted media:** a photo or video on a `--paper` mat with `print-*` shadow, resting at a slight angle. Keeps its real proportions and background; no text over faces.
- **Ticket:** `--ticket-stock` with a torn end, dashed perforation, `kicker` metadata and a Cooper date or title. A solid accent end with `--on-accent` lettering may carry the action.
- **Slip:** a torn strip of `--paper-shade` behind a link or note, at a resting angle.
- **Fastening:** tape holds an edge and lets the paper flex; a pin is a pivot the paper can swing on; corner mounts keep it flat. Loose prints lift on hover and focus; fastened ones don't.
- **Rotating object:** a card or object that turns in 3D or WebGL. It needs a flat fallback, native keyboard actions, a text description and live, level copies of any printed information. Device tilt is optional, permission-based, bounded and easy to turn off.

No task ever needs a drag, a sensor or a 3D object to complete.

## Motion

| Mode         | Motion                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Working**  | `fast` color and opacity changes, `lift` on hover, `base` slides for menus and panels, all on `ease-out`. Nothing that delays input.                                           |
| **Reading**  | Same as Working, plus a `base` cross-fade between pages.                                                                                                                       |
| **Showcase** | Physical gestures between `gesture-min` and `gesture-max`: prints lifting and straightening, swings on a pin, a ticket end flexing, a one-time arrival of paper on first load. |

- **Route-continuity transition:** when one element clearly persists between two views (a photo becoming a header, a card becoming a page), move it over `transition` while the rest cross-fades over `base`. Native navigation, back/forward and scroll restoration keep working, and the transition can be interrupted.
- **Reduced motion:** remove travel, tilt, flex and arrivals; keep state changes as instant or opacity-only.
- **Input wins:** keyboard focus or any input settles an animation immediately.
- **Performance:** pause offscreen and hidden rendering, coalesce pointer updates, release listeners and render resources on navigation. No scroll hijacking or parallax that delays content. Content works without JavaScript.

## Iconography

Outline icons at `icon` size with an `icon-stroke` stroke, rounded joins, `currentColor`. Icons sit beside a word, or have an accessible name when alone. Don't mix icon families or switch to filled icons for emphasis; use the accent or a label instead.

## Imagery

- Real photographs only, with real proportions and backgrounds. No cut-out portraits, stock imagery as decoration, or generated pictures presented as real.
- In Showcase, images are mounted objects. In Working and Reading they sit flat with `sm` corners and a caption in `meta`.
- Hand-drawn annotation is welcome only if a person actually drew and scanned it. It annotates something, never covers a face, text or link, is never a state or navigation color, is decorative to assistive tech and hides in forced colors. No imitations in SVG, CSS or generated art; if the drawing doesn't exist, leave it out.
- Every meaningful image has alt text; decorative ones have an empty `alt`.

## Voice and microcopy

Plain, warm and direct. Short sentences. Say what something does ("Save changes", "Copy link"), not what it is ("Submit", "OK"). Errors explain what went wrong and what to do next, without blame. Confirmations repeat the action. Theatrical words are fine in Showcase headings; controls and messages stay literal everywhere.

## Data visualization

Charts are Working-mode content. Use `--ink` for primary series, `--muted` for comparison and gridlines on `--border`. For categories, use at most five colors in this order: accent, info, success, important, warning. Beyond five, group, facet or label directly. Never rely on color alone: add direct labels, patterns or markers. Numbers use PragmataPro with tabular figures. No 3D, gradients or shadows on charts.

## Empty, loading and error states

- **Empty:** say what will appear here and offer the action that fills it. A small fleuron or drawing is fine in Reading and Showcase; Working keeps it to text and one button.
- **Loading:** reserve the final layout with `--paper-shade` blocks; animate opacity only (none under reduced motion). Announce long waits with a polite live region.
- **Error:** an alert in the danger state with what failed, why if known, and a retry or way out. Keep anything the person entered.
- **Success:** brief, in place, and announced; don't steal focus.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use semantic roles, never edition literals, in components.
- Pick a surface mode for each screen and use that mode's subset of the system.
- Let real tasks decide measure, density, navigation and hierarchy.
- Support keyboard use, visible focus, text enlargement, reduced motion and forced colors. In forced colors, drop decorative layers and keep system-color outlines.
- Test both offered editions, real fonts and fallbacks, narrow and wide layouts, long content and every state.

### Don't

- Don't treat Showcase vocabulary as required. Tools don't need tickets, drop caps or 3D to belong.
- Don't use Cooper for body text or controls, or mono to dress ordinary UI as a terminal.
- Don't replace warm stock with pure black, pure white, cool greys, neon, black-and-gold glitz or gradient cards.
- Don't shadow lettering, rules, icons or ink, or tilt live text.
- Don't let a faint rule be the only way to find a control, or color be the only way to read a state.

## Adoption checklist

1. Record the app's theme policy (daylight, evening or both, and the default).
2. Confirm font licensing and CORS cover the app's domain.
3. Choose the surface mode for each main screen.
4. Map components to semantic roles; add app-specific tokens locally.
5. Check contrast for any pair not in the table above, including tints, overlays and syntax.
6. Test keyboard, focus, 200% text, reduced motion and forced colors.
7. Test empty, loading, error and success states.

## Open decisions

- **Evening code stock.** `#282828` matches Gruvbox but is a neutral grey, which bends the Warm Stock principle. A warmer value such as `#26221c` would keep the principle but drift from the syntax theme.
